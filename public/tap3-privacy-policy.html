1. Our privacy commitments

TAP3 Technologies (“TAP3,” “we,” “our,” or “us”) develops and operates business software, communications and customer relationship tools, point-of-sale and workforce systems, billing and accounting tools, information technology and security offerings, artificial-intelligence-assisted services, and certain public-safety technologies. We recognize that many of our customers entrust these systems with business information and, depending on the product, information about their customers, employees, guests, patients, visitors, or members of the public.

The central principles of this policy are that personal information should be collected for explained and legitimate purposes; access should be limited to people and systems with a need to use it; information from one unrelated customer or legal entity should not be disclosed to another merely because both use TAP3; individuals should have meaningful choices where required by law; and sensitive records require safeguards appropriate to their risk and legal restrictions.

TAP3 uses this common privacy framework across applicable products, together with product-specific disclosures, notices displayed when information is collected, contracts, and any required agency or industry agreements. If a customer uses a separately branded deployment, the operator and controller identified in that deployment's own notice may be different from TAP3.

2. Who this policy covers and how the parties' roles differ

TAP3 as controller/business. TAP3 determines the purposes and means of processing personal information for its own corporate website; product-interest forms; creation and administration of TAP3 customer accounts; subscriptions, direct invoices and payments owed to TAP3; TAP3 marketing; vendor and sales contacts; fraud prevention; platform operations; customer support; and corporate security. This policy describes those activities.

TAP3 as processor/service provider. A restaurant using TAP3 POS to serve its patrons, an employer using Restaurant Workforce, a business using RankFlo to contact customers, or an agency using Digital Police ordinarily decides what information is collected, why it is needed, who may access it, how long it is retained, and when it may be shared. For those records, that organization generally acts as controller/business and TAP3 processes data on its documented behalf, subject to the parties' agreement and applicable law. The customer's own privacy notice and appropriate terms also apply.

Independent brands and customer deployments. Culinary Creations Group, a TAP-branded restaurant, Brewster Products, Inc., other independent companies, and white-label customers must each identify their own correct legal operator when they determine their own processing purposes. A shared login, branding relationship, or hosted application does not automatically merge their rights to customer information. This master policy may be referenced by such operators to describe TAP3's technology-provider role, but it does not replace their notices.

Government and law enforcement. Records in an agency-controlled environment—including criminal justice information, official evidence, personnel files, and active investigations—are handled under the agency's authority, applicable law, its records/evidence schedules, and the controlling agreements. This policy does not give a member of the public a direct right to have official police evidence erased and does not authorize TAP3 to release restricted case information.

Priority of terms. Where a product-specific notice or law provides greater protections, those provisions apply for that product. A valid data processing agreement, business associate agreement, agency agreement, or mandatory legal requirement may prescribe a different process for records that TAP3 handles on a customer's behalf. No contract or notice can override applicable law.

5. Where information comes from

We may receive information (a) directly from people who register, inquire, purchase, consent, contact support, or use the service; (b) from businesses, agencies, and authorized administrators who create accounts or upload records; (c) automatically from devices, browsers, and applications, including security and technical logs; (d) from integrations selected and authorized by a customer, such as payment providers, merchant systems, email/SMS services, financial-connectivity services, identity providers, websites, and payroll providers; (e) from connected security devices, cameras, vehicle-scanning equipment, or approved agency systems; and (f) from public, licensed, or permitted data sources when a particular feature lawfully uses them.

The organization operating a campaign, camera site, workforce program, or police system is responsible for obtaining lawful authority for its collection and for giving legally required notices to the affected individuals. A customer's ability to upload information does not mean it is legally permitted to upload it.

Where a customer controls the data, TAP3's permitted processing purposes are limited by the customer's lawful instructions and the relevant contract. We do not treat a merchant's guest list, an employer's employee roster, or an agency's case records as general-purpose TAP3 advertising lists simply because that information is stored using a TAP3 system.

Incompatible new uses. Where a materially different use is proposed, we or the responsible customer must provide the required additional notice and obtain consent or another lawful authorization before that use begins. A general acceptance of terms does not replace consent where a more specific consent is legally required.

7. Sensitive information and additional restrictions

Certain information requires heightened protection, including sensitive personal data defined by state law; financial account credentials; national or state identification numbers; precise location; protected health records; biometric identifiers used to recognize a person; criminal justice information; law-enforcement investigations and evidence; and information about children, victims, witnesses, or other vulnerable individuals.

Processing of such information is restricted to the particular service purpose for which it is provided, an authorized role, a lawful basis, and a suitable security configuration. The responsible operator must assess whether consent, additional notices, age-based restrictions, a data protection assessment, a special contract, or a dedicated regulated environment is required. Users should not place full payment card numbers, card security codes, bank passwords, Social Security numbers, criminal justice information, or patient records into ordinary marketing, support-chat, or general AI prompts unless a feature is specifically authorized and secured for that purpose.

New facial recognition, biometric matching, live microphone monitoring, precise background location tracking, and other enhanced surveillance capabilities require a documented review before activation. The presence of a camera or AI option does not alone authorize facial identification, unrestricted audio recording, or use unrelated to the customer's lawful purpose.

10. AI, automation, generative assistance and algorithmic alerts

Some TAP3 products use or may offer AI-assisted drafting, classification, search, information extraction, recommendations, customer support, financial summaries, customer segmentation, document processing, image/video analysis, and alerts. AI features may create new derived information from inputs and may be inaccurate, incomplete, delayed, or biased. Users must review outputs as appropriate to the risk. AI assistance is not itself a medical, legal, accounting, investment, employment, or law-enforcement determination.

An organization must authorize the categories of information submitted to AI and confirm that the selected deployment and provider are permitted to process it. Police evidence, criminal justice information, financial credentials, protected health information, and other restricted materials must not be routed to an unapproved general-purpose model. AI providers may be subprocessors when expressly approved under applicable contracts, configuration, and access restrictions. Provider retention, human review, content logging, training use, and location of processing must be determined and disclosed before activation.

Proposed TAP3 rule for customer data: Customer confidential content, police evidence, body-camera video, criminal justice information, and employee personnel records must not be used to train a shared general-purpose AI model or to create an unrelated customer's marketing model unless expressly lawful, separately authorized, and clearly disclosed. This rule must be enforced by technical settings and vendor contracts before publication as an existing fact.

Human oversight: Decisions that could materially affect someone's employment, compensation, access to financial services, reputation, liberty, public safety, or rights must be made or reviewed by an appropriately authorized person, with available records of material inputs and action where required. Official police reports and supplements are to be authored and approved by the responsible officer; AI may help with research, structure, proofreading and reminders, not independently make the official factual assertion or finalize the report. A gun-detection or plate-match alert is a lead requiring appropriate human verification, not proof that a weapon or wanted vehicle is present.

14. Surveillance, video, audio, cameras and connected security devices

Camera and security products can capture people, premises, behavior, audio if enabled, vehicle information, locations, timestamps, device health, alerts, and operator actions. 15. License-plate recognition, vehicle data and location

LPR features may process plate characters, captured vehicle photographs, make/model/color estimates, date and time, camera/device identifiers, GPS or site location, and alert/match results. Vehicle metadata and plate information may identify individuals when combined with other sources, even when a plate alone is not always personally identifying. Collection should be limited to an authorized purpose and location.

Each deployment must define who can search and share plate records; the permissible sources and quality of hot lists; how false matches are confirmed; whether an agency or business is legally permitted to retain or disclose the information; and the default retention and export rules. Where required, criminal-justice or motor-vehicle record restrictions apply. Data must not be made publicly searchable or sold to data brokers by default. Access to another agency's plate records requires the relevant agency authorization and agreement.

Mobile LPR or location functions should request device location only when the permitted feature requires it, display needed device permissions, and document whether scanning continues in the background. Any direct vehicle identification or enforcement action should consider accuracy, policy and human verification.

16. Digital Police, evidence and criminal justice records

The customer law-enforcement agency is responsible for the legal basis, custody, authorized users, release decisions, retention, evidentiary process and records schedules associated with its operational data. Such records may include officer credentials, case documents, digital evidence, victim and witness information, criminal justice information, vehicle and location data, authorized investigative leads, camera recordings and audit events. These records require more restrictive handling than ordinary commercial customer information.

TAP3 must not treat agency case information as shared consumer marketing data; must not independently release agency evidence based on an ordinary customer-service request; and must not merge records from unrelated agencies without lawful authority. Any transfer to courts, another agency, investigators, evidentiary exports or integrated systems should be traceable to an authorized user, policy or legal process. Evidence integrity, timestamps, access history, chain of custody, hold status and audit records must be maintained in accordance with deployment requirements.

An agency using CJI must verify necessary security controls, personnel and vendor obligations, state-specific approvals and agreements, and the applicable current FBI CJIS Security Policy before production use. This document is not an assurance that the proposed TAP3 private cloud, a third-party host, or any specific module is presently approved for CJI. Testing should use synthetic or lawfully de-identified data until the required authorization is obtained.

FOIA/public-records questions, subpoenas, discovery, expungement, sealing, juvenile confidentiality, victim privacy and evidence disclosure are handled by the responsible agency or relevant lawful authority. Ordinary account deletion does not remove evidence under legal hold or bypass preservation and disclosure obligations.

20. Record retention, deletion, backup and legal holds

Records are retained only for periods justified by the purpose, documented contract, lawful records schedule, security requirements, tax or accounting obligations, dispute management, and relevant preservation requirements. A company or agency may have records that must be held even after an individual user leaves. The exact periods must be reflected in the configured retention schedules of each deployed product; generic promises of immediate deletion are not appropriate.

Typical retention factors include: account and contract duration; a reasonable period for dispute defense; statutory financial and payroll rules; consent and suppression-list evidence; defined log review periods; customer-selected video and LPR schedules; and official records/evidence disposition rules. A customer's termination request must distinguish between export of the customer's data, suspension of access, scheduled deletion of accessible primary copies, backup expiration, preservation of minimal security records, and legal holds.

Deletion, de-identification, restricted archival or destruction is carried out when permitted and required. Immutable backups or evidence preservation systems may expire under documented cycles rather than instantly. Data subject to litigation hold, court order, audit, government investigation, legal retention or criminal discovery may be preserved until release is authorized. When a retention hold ends, normal disposal rules should resume.

21. Security practices and incident notification

TAP3's intended security program uses risk-based administrative, technical and physical protections. Depending on the deployment, controls may include strong authentication, multifactor authentication, least-privilege access, secure transport, encryption of appropriate data at rest, key management, device controls, segregated environments, security logging, monitoring, incident response, tested recovery procedures, service-provider oversight, and periodic security review. A public statement about any specific safeguard is accurate only when the safeguard actually operates for the relevant product.

Customers are responsible for protecting their own usernames, devices, employees, access assignments, configuration and lawful use. They should report suspected compromise through the product's published security channel. We may investigate incidents, restrict access, preserve necessary records, assist affected controllers and give legally required notice as appropriate to our role. Under Delaware's breach notification law, certain qualifying incidents require resident notice without unreasonable delay and generally within 60 days after discovery, subject to statutory conditions and exceptions; notification obligations differ for other states and sectors.

No internet-connected platform can guarantee the prevention of every breach or loss. Any claim of CJIS authorization, PCI DSS compliance, HIPAA compliance or SOC 2 examination must refer to the actual assessed product, deployment and evidence, not simply this privacy policy.

22. Children's information and age-related protections

TAP3's ordinary business administration products are intended for adult operators and authorized professional users, not for children under 13 to create independent consumer accounts. A business or agency may nonetheless enter records concerning minors for a lawful service, workforce, school, investigative or other permitted purpose; the responsible organization must meet its additional legal duties. For a child-directed product, or where TAP3 knowingly collects children's personal information directly, required notices, parental permissions and safeguards must be implemented before collection.

Where law imposes specific restrictions for ages 13 through 17, such as restrictions on targeted advertising, sales or sensitive processing, the responsible operator must honor them. We will not assume a minor's data is freely available for marketing or AI training because an adult or organization uploaded it.

TAP3's initial product focus is the United States, but vendors, infrastructure, support personnel, and integrations may be located elsewhere depending on the configuration. International availability should not be advertised until these checks are complete.

An updated policy will not retroactively legalize a new use of restricted or sensitive information when affirmative authorization is required. We will keep a version history suitable for audit and customer support.

PRODUCT-SPECIFIC PRIVACY SUPPLEMENTS

The following sections supplement the master notice. They apply only when the described product and feature is actually deployed. They are not a substitute for customer agreements, operator signage, employee disclosures, agency records policies, or app-store disclosures.

Supplement A — TAP3 ONE: billing, accounting, contracts and business management

Responsible party: TAP3 for its own customer account and subscription details; the subscribing business for its customer, vendor, employee and commercial records. Typical records: Business registration details, EIN or other tax identifiers, authorized users, invoices, receipts, expenses, purchase orders, inventory items, vendor information, contracts, payment status, signed documents, recurring subscriptions, finance reports, bank connections and accounting correspondence. Purposes: Business administration, financial reporting, invoicing, subscription billing, expense management, inventory, recordkeeping and authorized integrations.

Separate company accounts and legal entities must have separate permissions, financial ledgers and data visibility. A parent-company management account may access only the entities it is properly authorized to supervise. Authorized accountant or bookkeeper access must be scoped by company and role and may be revoked. Signing an agreement electronically may generate timestamps, identities, device metadata and an evidence record. Accounting AI should explain its source records and uncertainties; it must not silently alter transaction values, execute financial transfers or purport to issue a certified financial opinion.

If financial account linking, payroll APIs or payment processing is enabled, the responsible organization must be shown the provider, data categories, connection purpose, authorization and revocation route. Sensitive credentials must be handled only through an appropriate secure provider flow. Long-term accounting, tax, payroll and contractual records may be subject to retention requirements after the subscription ends.

Supplement B — TAP3 POS, online ordering, handhelds, KDS and merchant checkout

A subscribing merchant may collect customer names, contact information, table or order history, special instructions, loyalty details, receipts, tender status, tips, delivery information and transaction metadata. Employee/operator records may include assigned register, PIN-related authentication events, drawer counts, shift records, tips, voids, discounts and audit logs. Those staff audit records are used for authorized operations, fraud reduction and accountability, not unrelated consumer marketing.

Payment-card handling must follow the actual merchant/acquirer/service-provider design and applicable standards. Receipts, order history and disputes may remain in merchant records subject to required recordkeeping. If a customer requests a receipt by email or text, that request is not an automatic opt-in for promotional messages. Table mapping, KDS timers and kitchen workflow data may reveal activity patterns but are not permissions for hidden workplace surveillance.

Supplement C — TAP3 CRM, RankFlo, loyalty, email and marketing automation

Business customers may maintain prospect lists, names, preferences, engagement records, service history, campaigns, appointment reminders, loyalty points, birthdays or anniversaries, website forms, chatbot conversations, and permitted segmentation. The business sending a message is the campaign owner and must establish an appropriate basis to collect and contact each person. Importing a list does not establish consent, and a purchase or customer inquiry does not by itself authorize automated promotional calls or texts.

Marketing tools should maintain campaign origin, sender name, form language shown at signup, consent timestamps, channel and source, suppression records, and opt-out status. A recipient's withdrawal must be reflected across the applicable sender's campaigns. RankFlo rewards and promotions must explain eligibility and how relevant information is used; participating in a loyalty program must not silently waive independent privacy rights. Where AI is allowed to create marketing sequences, it must follow authorized audiences and opt-outs rather than infer permission.

Supplement D — Guest Wi-Fi registration, captive portals and websites

Guest Wi-Fi and web-registration features may receive the guest's name, email or mobile number if requested; approximate network location; device or network identifiers; connection times; consent choices; and access events. The site operator must distinguish necessary guest-network access information from optional marketing enrollment. A person must not be automatically subscribed to promotional SMS merely by accepting Wi-Fi terms or connecting a device.

Website analytics, cookies and pixels must be disclosed accurately for the particular site, with choices where applicable. Business customers must ensure their Verizon, Comcast or other networking equipment and captive portal configuration can actually carry out the stated notices and suppression rules. The network provider and the business may each have separate responsibilities.

Supplement E — Restaurant Workforce: scheduling, hiring, HR and employee communications

An employer may process applicants' names, qualifications, work history, onboarding documents, work eligibility status, training records, handbook acknowledgments, schedules, shift changes, availability, time-off requests, clock events, tip reporting, employee chat, certifications and work-related incident notes. The employer should give a specific workforce privacy notice stating what monitoring, location, video, messaging or payroll features are actually enabled, rather than relying solely on the public customer notice.

Management and employee roles should separate applicants, coworkers, HR records, financial details and disciplinary material. A person should ordinarily see their own schedule and profile, but not another employee's sensitive file. Integration to POS for sales, tips and hours must be authorized by the employer. Mandatory workplace messages should be distinguished from optional marketing; employee telephone numbers must not be automatically exported to unrelated promotional campaigns. Employment-retention and access rules apply even where consumer privacy statutes exempt ordinary employment data.

Supplement F — Catering, event management and independent hospitality brands

A caterer or event operator may collect client contact details, event addresses, guest counts, dietary requests, booking deposits, event timelines, invoices, menu choices, delivery information, contracts and signatures. Some dietary requests may imply sensitive health or religious information; such details should be limited to the event purpose and shared only with staff or contractors who need them to provide the requested service. Payment and refund records are subject to the relevant merchant and retention arrangements.

If the service is operated by Culinary Creations Group or another separate company, the customer-facing policy must clearly identify that company as the operator and TAP3 as technology provider only to the extent accurate. A wholesale/reseller partner portal must prevent one reseller from seeing another partner's confidential pricing, customer list or event records.

Supplement G — MSP, endpoint security, IT services and customer support

Managed technology services may record endpoints, hardware identifiers, network events, threat detections, support tickets, technician actions, device configurations and administrator contacts. Staff access to customer systems must be approved and limited to support, monitoring, security or deployment functions under the customer's agreement. If remote viewing, mailbox access, file collection or security investigation is required, the activity must be separately authorized and logged where appropriate.

Regulated customers may require extra agreements, access rules and personnel screening. Customer support logs must not be used to populate advertising audiences. Security telemetry may require restricted retention during incident investigations or threat analysis, subject to relevant contracts and law.

Supplement H — Cloud Video, cameras and private-cloud recording

Video services may store live and recorded streams, camera names and placements, timestamps, user activity, alarm events, health data and clips exported by authorized users. Depending on equipment and configuration, audio, motion, analytics or location may also be captured. The camera operator is responsible for deciding where cameras are installed and how persons are notified, obtaining audio permissions when required, and establishing the purpose and retention schedule.

TAP3 must limit support access, maintain evidence of authorized export where appropriate, prevent casual cross-tenant access, and respect tenant-specific deletion and retention instructions. Video shared for a claimed incident is not public evidence merely because the platform can export it. A customer who requests private-cloud hosting may have specific physical-server, data-center or sovereignty terms that must be documented separately.

Supplement I — License plate recognition and vehicle intelligence

LPR systems may collect license plate images/text, vehicle photos, make/model/color predictions, device GPS, timestamp, scan location, hot-list entries and alert history. The authorized operator is responsible for lawful use of the scanner, accuracy review, retention schedule and who may run queries. The platform should audit searches and disclosure to agencies and not provide open consumer lookup of collected plates.

Alerts may be wrong because of lighting, plate similarity, source-list problems or data age. A plate hit is a notification for human follow-up, not a definitive identity or criminal finding. Any integration with state motor vehicle or criminal justice information must independently satisfy applicable licensing, purpose limits and approval requirements.

Supplement J — ThreatTrack or weapons-detection video analytics

Weapons-detection features may process camera footage, frame samples, incident location, alert events, device information and authorized reviewer actions. The intended analysis is weapon detection—not identification of a person's identity by face. The system can have false positives or false negatives and must not be presented as an emergency-response guarantee. Customer operators remain responsible for site notifications, incident responses and decision-making. A new face recognition or person-tracking function requires its own privacy and legal review before being enabled.

Supplement K — Body-worn cameras, in-car video and evidence management

A body-camera or in-car deployment may capture video, audio, GPS, officer or vehicle identifiers, event metadata, upload status, evidence identifiers, chain-of-custody information, access logs and exported records. Agency policy and applicable law govern activation, sensitive recordings, tagging, access, evidence review, redaction, export, retention and release. Consumer account-deletion tools do not authorize destruction of preserved evidence.

The use of TAP3-branded hardware does not change who owns the agency's evidence or authorize the hardware maker or AI provider to access it. Cloud upload, local buffering, device loss, tampering, offline storage, audit history and synchronization policies need a documented deployment-specific agreement and technical verification.

Supplement L — Digital Police: patrol, investigations, administration and AI assistance

Digital Police features may process agency policy materials, public statutes, incident metadata, officer notes, case-related documents, court preparation material, authorized digital evidence, incident maps, training records and organizational communications. Agency administrators must determine the appropriate user roles and permissible integrations. Imported CAD/RMS, DELJIS, criminal history or other restricted information may be used only after agency and system-specific authority is established.

AI-assisted Patrol and Detective tools provide research, checklists, analytic suggestions, evidence search and optional text-improvement support. They must not invent facts, independently author officer-certified incident reports or bypass agency approval for an investigative action. Administrative AI and assistants may prepare drafts, reminders, scheduling suggestions and communications, but a human authorized officer or employee must approve consequential or official external actions. The agency controls record retention, disclosure, court orders, discovery and preservation requirements.

Supplement M — Command Center and cross-platform integrations

Cross-platform operation may exchange service status, subscriber details, payment state, authorized contacts, product health, approved analytics and permitted support records. It does not authorize automatic copying of every customer database into one shared searchable index. Each integration should declare source system, destination, categories of data, owner, lawful purpose, permissible administrator roles, retention and revocation method.

If a customer activates a connection to an outside provider or to another TAP3 product, the system must respect the originating system's access restrictions and the specific customer permission. A support incident may require access to limited logs without access to raw financial, personnel or evidence records. Audit events may be preserved for security and accountability.

Supplement N — Third-party and white-label client applications

Where TAP3 provides a standalone platform built for another company—for example, a Brewster Products, Inc. service-management system—the customer organization is generally responsible for the information it enters about service customers, equipment purchases, technician visits, machine serial numbers, maintenance history, delivery records, invoices, signatures and contracts. The business should publish its own notice and name TAP3 as vendor/processor as appropriate. Data belonging to that business may not be commingled with other TAP3 customers or disclosed to competing service providers without lawful authorization.

Supplement O — Future products, integrations and materially new uses

Before launching a new TAP3 product, AI agent, payroll integration, camera feature, loyalty program, messaging program or additional data sharing, the responsible product owner should update the inventory of personal data, recipients, role assignments, lawful basis, consent requirements, retention and security controls. A privacy notice or supplement must be revised where the new feature changes what individuals reasonably understand about how their information is processed. The master policy is intended to be reusable, not a substitute for deployment-by-deployment review.
